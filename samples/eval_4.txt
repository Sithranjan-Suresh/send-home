HOSPITAL COURSE
The patient presented with marked hyperglycemia, polyuria, and dysuria. Urinalysis was consistent with a urinary tract infection. Management consisted of intravenous crystalloid resuscitation, a subcutaneous insulin regimen, and empiric antimicrobial therapy. Glycemic control has improved to an acceptable range for outpatient management.

DISCHARGE MEDICATIONS
Metformin 500 mg tablet: take 1 tablet by mouth twice daily with meals, including on days when oral intake is reduced.
Glargine insulin: inject 20 units subcutaneously once daily at bedtime. Perform a fingerstick glucose before each dose. If your sugar is below 100, call the clinic before taking your insulin.
Ciprofloxacin 500 mg tablet: take 1 tablet by mouth every 12 hours for 3 days. Do not take it within 2 hours of antacids or dairy products, which impair absorption.
Glucose tablets: chew 4 tablets if your blood sugar is below 70.

SELF-MANAGEMENT
Perform self-monitoring of blood glucose 4 times a day: before meals and at bedtime. Record every result in a log. Maintain adequate hydration throughout the day. Adhere to a carbohydrate-consistent diet with reduced simple sugars and starches, and eat meals at regular intervals.

CONTACT YOUR PRIMARY CARE PROVIDER IF
Your blood sugar is over 300 two times in a row, you have burning with urination that is not better in 2 days, or you have a fever over 100.4 degrees F.

ACTIVATE 911 OR PROCEED TO THE EMERGENCY DEPARTMENT IF
You are very sleepy or confused, you have fruity-smelling breath with nausea and vomiting, you pass out, or your blood sugar stays below 70 after you have treated it twice (severe hypoglycemia).

APPOINTMENTS
Primary care in 5 days for a blood sugar log review.
Diabetes education class: the clinic will call you with a date.
Dilated eye exam within 3 months.
