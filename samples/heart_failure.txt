Diagnosis: Congestive heart failure flare (fluid build-up because the heart is not pumping as strongly as it should).

WHAT HAPPENED
You came in with swelling in both legs, weight gain, and trouble breathing when lying flat. You were given a water pill through the IV and about 6 pounds of extra fluid was removed. Your heart tests showed no new heart attack.

YOUR MEDICINES
1. Furosemide 40 mg tablet: take 1 tablet by mouth every morning. This is a water pill.
2. Lisinopril 10 mg tablet: take 1 tablet by mouth once daily. Your doctor will check your kidneys and potassium at your first visit.
3. Metoprolol succinate: take by mouth once daily. Your doctor will tell you the dose at your first clinic visit.
4. Potassium chloride 20 mEq: take 1 tablet by mouth once daily with food.
CHANGES: Stop taking ibuprofen and naproxen. These can make your body hold on to water.

DAILY SELF-CARE
Weigh yourself every morning after you use the bathroom and before you eat. Write the number down. Eat less than 2,000 mg of sodium each day. Limit fluids to 64 ounces (about 8 cups) per day. Raise your legs when sitting. Walk a little each day and rest when you feel tired.

CALL YOUR CARE TEAM IF
You gain more than 3 pounds in one day or 5 pounds in one week. Call if your legs, ankles, or belly swell more than usual, if you need more pillows to sleep, if you feel dizzy when you stand, or if you have a dry cough that will not go away.

CALL 911 OR GO TO THE EMERGENCY ROOM IF
You have chest pain or pressure, severe shortness of breath at rest, fainting, a very fast or irregular heartbeat, or you cough up pink, foamy mucus.

Heart failure clinic with Dr. Okafor within 7 days. Bring your weight log and all of your pill bottles.
